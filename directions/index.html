Three ways this product could look, rendered rather than described.

Each one shows the same report, built from the same fixture data, so the comparison is about composition rather than content. They are not three themes of one layout: the information is ordered differently in each, because that is the decision actually being made.

Direction A

The Dossier

The report as a prepared document. A cover page, roman-numbered sections, provenance kept in the margin, and a ruled ledger instead of a chart. Reads like something a professional would be handed, not something a tool generated.

  • Serif throughout, print discipline
  • Evidence quoted, margin notes carry the method
  • Strongest when printed or sent as a PDF

Direction B

The Console

The report as an analyst’s workspace. A persistent rail, a stat strip across the top, dense tables and monospaced provenance. Built for someone who will run many of these and compare them.

  • Dark by default, light supported
  • Scannable at speed, high information density
  • Strongest for staff review and repeat use
Open direction B
Direction C

The Briefing

The report as a briefing. An ink cover, one number alone on the page, then findings one at a time — each ending in what it means. The score stops being the product and becomes the opening of an argument.

  • Deep-green ground, one accent used once
  • Narrative order, ends in a plan
  • Strongest for a first-time reader and a sale
Open direction C

What these are, and what they are not

Every figure, result and excerpt is fixture data describing a fictional attorney. No live search provider is connected to this product, so nothing here is a real finding about anybody. Each direction says so on its own page.

These are compositions, not a built product. Picking one is the decision; implementing it against the real engine is the work that follows. The current shipped site is a fourth thing — the build-status report shows what it looks like today and what is still wrong with it.
